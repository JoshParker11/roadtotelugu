Recap — every tense in two tables

Everything from Lessons 8 to 12 fits on one page. Two tables of suffixes, six stem classes, and one rule: the person marker mirrors the last syllable of the pronoun. Learn the tables and a new verb costs you a stem, not a paradigm.

This is the page to come back to. It is also the page that caught the biggest error in the Verb Lab so far.

How I processed this

Video 22 walks eight verbs cell by cell for thirty-one minutes — about 400 spoken forms, and the captions render most of them as European place names. The lesson also links a PDF, Telugu Verb Conjugations (bhashafy.com), 18 verbs over 36 pages.

Rather than transcribe by ear, I parsed the PDF (tools/parse_conjugations.py, 880 cells) and diffed all of it against the Verb Lab (tools/check_conjugations.py). Every table below is generated from the audited data, not typed out. The findings are in the audit section.

Read the negative table shape, not its contents

Two of its three columns are flat — one form for all eight pronouns. Only the future/habit column inflects. So the negative costs you eight forms per verb, not twenty-four, and two of the three tenses are a single word each.

That is the single most useful structural fact in week 2: when you are unsure of a positive form, negate the sentence.

The one rule underneath all of it

The person marker is the last syllable of the pronoun. nēnu → -ānu. nuvvu → -āvu. vāḍu → -āḍu. mīru → -āru.

It holds for six of the eight rows outright. The exceptions are worth naming because they are the same exceptions in every tense:

RowExpectedActualWhy
she / it-adi-indi · -tōndi · -tundiContracts. It is the most-used cell in the language — feminine and non-human share it.
she / it, negative future-adi-aduWould be identical to the pronoun adi sitting next to it.
avi (they, non-human)-avi-āyi · negative -avuSimply irregular. Its negative collides with nuvvu.
What actually varies

Six stem classes

The suffixes never change. The stem does, and there are only six ways it can. Each table below is the same grid as above with a different verb in it.

The verb that breaks the rules

uṇḍu — be, stay, exist

The video calls it "the only verb that breaks many of the rules of tenses we have learnt", and explains why: unnānu and the tense marker -tunnā- are the same shape. The conjugations of uṇḍu are where all the other suffixes came from. A verb cannot be regular against a pattern it generated.

lēdu is not one word

For every other verb the negative past is a single invariant form. Here it takes person markers — lēnu · lēvu · lēḍu · lēdu · lēmu · lēru — and these are high-frequency. lēru is what you get told on the phone: "they're not in."

The Verb Lab had this flat. Caught by this lesson; see below.

Cross-check

880 cells from the PDF, diffed against the generator

The largest check available: 18 verbs × 3 tenses × 2 polarities × 8 persons, parsed positionally and compared row by row.

Method — compare rows, not cells

A first pass compared cell against cell and reported 375 disagreements, nearly all of them four systematic things wearing 375 faces. A Telugu form is a stem plus an ending and those fail independently: a wrong stem breaks six cells while every ending stays perfect. Splitting the two gives 612 comparable cells in 102 rows, of which 69 rows are clean outright and 25 more differ only in the stem.

✗ The finding: lēdu inflects, and we had it flat

The PDF and the video agree: nēnu lēnu · nuvvu lēvu · vāḍu lēḍu · mēmu lēmu · mīru lēru. verbs.js carried a single override, lēdu, for all six persons.

Fixed. The override now accepts a six-cell array, and invariantFor() tells the paradigm tables to stop printing "↑ unchanged" for this one verb. Every other verb's negative past is genuinely invariant and still renders that way.

△ Where the PDF is wrong and we are right

Missing gemination. It prints ich-ānu, vach-āmu, kūrchun-ānu where Telugu has ichchānu, vachchāmu, kūrchunnānu.

Vowel length in the she/it present. Four verbs print -ṭondi where the PDF's own summary table says -tōndi.

Find-and-replace damage. Four pages were made by cloning the Play page and replacing "āḍu" with the new root — and the replace ran over the body text. On the Drink page the pronoun Vāḍu prints as "Vtāgu" and the he-row ending -āḍu as "-tāgu". Nine cells, detected by putting "āḍu" back.

△ Where we differ on purpose

The -va- glide. PDF kūrchō-ḍam-lēdu, ours kūrchōvaḍaṁ lēdu. Both are heard; ours is the standard written form. Four verbs. Worth a question.

"We". PDF -āmu from manamu, Verb Lab -āṁ from manaṁ. Careful vs colloquial. 49 cells, on record.

Nasal assimilation. PDF kūrchun-ṭānu, ours kūrchuṇṭānu — the anusvara before a retroflex is a retroflex nasal, which is what te2rom.py produces. Notation, not pronunciation.
