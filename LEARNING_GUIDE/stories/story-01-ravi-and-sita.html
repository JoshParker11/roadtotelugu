Ravi and Sita

A deliberately tiny classroom scene. It recycles Lesson 1’s near/far pronouns, identity sentences, plural forms, and human/object distinction while adding one new item: kūḍā, “also/too.”

Exempt labels: Ravi and Sita are proper names, not vocabulary targets.

kūḍāకూడా

Meaning: also, too

Place it after the person or thing being added: Sīta kūḍā vidyārthi, “Sita is also a student.” For this first story, treat that placement as a memorized frame; its wider behavior can be taught later.

Repeated form exposure

Notice families, not isolated strings.

The story intentionally rotates known forms. These are not counted as separate mystery words because Lesson 1 taught the distinction or transformation.

Near ↔ far · peopleitanu ↔ atanu
īme ↔ āme
vīḷḷu ↔ vāḷḷu

The opening i/ī points near; a/ā points farther away.

Near ↔ far · objectsidi ↔ adi
ivi ↔ avi

Singular and plural object forms repeat in parallel.

Singular → pluralvidyārthi → vidyārthulu
kāru → kārlu

The plural forms are deliberately re-encountered in complete identity sentences.

Repetition ladder

Five passes, five different jobs

Repeated reading is useful when each pass asks your brain to do more than stare at the same text. Stop while attention is still good; return later rather than forcing twenty numb repetitions.

01Supported meaning

Read romanization and open every meaning. Identify near/far and singular/plural.

02Close English

Collapse meanings. Recover the scene line by line; reopen only after committing to an answer.

03Notice forms

Track each pronoun pair and plural. Say what changed before reading the next line.

04Listen and point

Once audio exists, point to imagined near/far people and objects while listening without text.

05Rebuild

Use only the English meanings or a simple sketch and produce each Telugu line aloud.

  • Who is a student?Ravi vidyārthi. Sīta kūḍā vidyārthi.
  • Produce: “These people are students.”vīḷḷu vidyārthulu.
  • Change near to far: idi kāru.adi kāru.
  • Change singular to plural: idi kāru.ivi kārlu.
  • Produce: “This lady is also a teacher.”īviḍa kūḍā ṭīcharu.
