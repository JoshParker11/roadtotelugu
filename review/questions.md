# Questions for a native speaker

Everything the extraction could not settle from the sources. Each was raised on a lesson page and
is repeated here so the whole list can be worked through in one sitting — ten minutes with a
fluent speaker clears most of it, and several of these block real decisions in the data.

Ordered by how much they matter, not by lesson.

---

## 1. Which prohibitive does she actually use? — `-vaddu` or `-ku`

*Lesson 7.* The Verb Lab generates `chēyaku` / `chēyakaṇḍi`; the course teaches `chēyavaddu`.
**Both are real and they are not identical** — `chēyaku` is a direct "don't do it", `chēyavaddu`
is closer to "it's not wanted / you shouldn't". `vaddu` is also the "no thanks" from Lesson 3.

**Why it matters:** two paradigms in the Lab, and I do not know which one to make the default.

## 2. Is "I'm not about to ___" expressible at all?

*Lesson 11.* The course teaches no negative for the immediate future, and the ordinary negative
future (`āḍanu`, "I won't play") appears to cover the ground. Is there a natural way to say it, or
does Telugu simply not make the distinction?

**Why it matters:** `immFuture` ships positive-only in the Verb Lab because of this.

## 3. "Sad" — `vichārangā` or `bādhagā`?

*Lesson 12.* The captions gave "vikramgad". `vichārangā` fits the sounds and is correct Telugu;
`bādhagā` is likely commoner in Hyderabad. The word is in the deck either way, so the question is
which one to put on the front.

## 4. The `-va-` glide: `kūrchōvaḍaṁ` or `kūrchōḍaṁ`?

*Lesson 13, from the PDF audit.* The conjugation PDF prints `kūrchō-ḍam-lēdu`; the generator
produces `kūrchōvaḍaṁ lēdu`. Both are heard; ours is the standard written form. Affects four
`-ō` verbs across every negative-present cell.

## 5. Six verb stems nobody has confirmed

*Lesson 15.* No source lists these; they were derived from the class patterns the audited verbs
establish. Reliable for the regular classes, a guess for these:

| verb | | the assumption |
|---|---|---|
| `ēḍuvu` | cry | three stems that share little: `ēḍus-` / `ēḍch-` / `ēḍava-` |
| `aruvu` | scream | past `arichānu` is certain; non-past also heard as `arustānu` |
| `neṭṭu` | push | assumed to soften ṭṭ → ḍ like `koṭṭu`, giving `neḍatānu`; `neṭṭutānu` also heard |
| `kālchu` | burn | `kālustānu` / `kālchānu` |
| `modaleṭṭu` | start | same `peṭṭu` softening — `modaleḍatānu` |
| `lekkapeṭṭu` | count | same |

Plus three flagged from the original 35: `parugettu`, `visiru`, `nadupu`, `eguru`.

**Why it matters:** these are `conf:'check'` in the Lab and surface on its review page. Each
confirmation unlocks a whole paradigm.

## 6. Two kinds of "have" — where is the line?

*Lesson 18.* Telugu has no verb "to have", and two constructions replace it:

- `nā daggara pilli undi` — locative. "Near me a cat exists."
- `nāku talanoppi undi` — dative. "To me a headache exists."

My guess is **`daggara` for things you could put down, `nāku` for things you cannot** — but the
course never draws the line and I am inferring it.

## 7. `pāpaḍu` for papad?

*Lesson 14.* The source gives `పాపడు`. The usual Telugu is `appaḍaṁ` (అప్పడం). Flagged rather
than changed, and currently held out of the deck.

## 8. Numbers just past 100

*Lesson 6.* The traditional combining form is `nūṭa` — 150 as *nūṭa yābhai*, 101 as *nūṭa okaṭi* —
but `vanda` is also heard directly. The one place in the number system where regional and
generational variation is likely.

## 9. `nuvva` — is it real?

*Earlier.* The book prints `నువ్వ` where the corpus overwhelmingly has `నువ్వు`. It was folded as
a misprint on corpus evidence, but a bound-stem reading is plausible (`nuvva` before some
suffixes). Currently merged away; worth a sanity check.

## Also worth asking, lower stakes

- **`mēmu` vs `manaṁ` in her actual speech.** The Verb Lab uses `manaṁ` / `-āṁ`; the course
  teaches `manamu` / `-āmu`. Register, not error — but which does she say?
- **Does `avi` (they, non-human) come up?** It is the one pronoun row the Verb Lab does not
  model. If it never appears in household speech, that stays correct.
- **`kyāreṭ` or `kyāreṭṭu`** for carrot, and whether `ఈరోజు` or `ఈ రోజు` is the normal spelling.

## The ongoing list

Everything above came out of the sources. **The more valuable list is the one that comes out of
your own mouth** — the "what I could not say" field in the daily log, and `log/inbox.md` once it
exists. Those are gaps in what you actually want to express, which no course can anticipate.

Bring both lists to the same conversation.
