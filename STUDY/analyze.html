Which words first

Paste a transcript, lyrics, anything in Telugu. It counts what is there, checks it against what you know, and tells you how far twenty more words would get you. Nothing is baked and nothing is uploaded — this runs in the page, so it can answer “is this worth my time” before you commit to ingesting it.

What this text says over and over

Two different findings. A fixed phrase is worth memorising as it stands. A frame is worth more than any sentence in it, because it generates rather than repeats — and the miner separates frames whose slot varies by word from ones whose slot varies by verb form, since the second kind is a conjugation the Verb Lab already drills.

Fixed phrases

Ranked by how many separate lines they turn up in — five occurrences across five lines is a phrase the speaker reaches for; five inside one line is a chorus.

Frames

Ranked by how many different things fill the slot. Nine fillers is a generator; two is a coincidence that happened twice.

Saved sources

Only the word counts are kept, never the text you pasted — the counts are the point, and the source may not be yours to store.

Across everything

Chunks in more than one source

The strongest signal the analyzer produces. A phrase two unrelated speakers both reach for is the language talking, not the topic.

How matching works here

Telugu script matches the master's script directly; romanization matches the folded romanization. Both are exact — unlike the reader, this page does no approximate matching, because a frequency table full of wrong glosses is worse than one with honest gaps. Inflected forms will therefore show as unknown even when you know the stem, which inflates the unknown count somewhat.

“Known” comes from the same store the reader and the vocabulary queue write, so all three agree about what you know.
