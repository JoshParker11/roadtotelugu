Ten questions per verb, not four hundred

Because the site stores stems and generates the forms, a native speaker never has to read a full conjugation table. Confirm four or five stems and the whole paradigm for that verb is settled at once — one wrong stem is one correction, not forty.

How to run it

The four questions that settle a verb

Ask them in Telugu-with-English, out loud, and write down what you actually hear rather than what you expected. Ten minutes covers five verbs.

01

“I will ___”

Gives you the non-past stem and the tense marker. If they say something other than what is printed, that one stem is wrong and every future, present-continuous and conditional form for the verb follows it.

02

“I ___ed” / “yesterday I ___”

Gives you the past stem — the one most likely to be irregular, and the one worth getting right first.

03

“I won’t ___” and “I didn’t ___”

Gives you the negative stem, which also drives both prohibitives.

04

“Please ___” and “I must ___”

Gives you the short-a stem and the infinitive. Between them these four questions pin down every cell on the site.

Ask about register while you are there

These paradigms assume educated colloquial Hyderabad/Telangana speech, which is what your course targets and almost certainly what your family speaks. A coastal speaker or a textbook will give you chebutānu where this site prints cheptānu, and -āmu where it prints -āṁ. Neither is wrong. Note which one you are hearing at home and follow that.
