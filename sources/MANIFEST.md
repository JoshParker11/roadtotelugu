## Ingest order

Script-bearing sources go first. Every source that has Telugu script feeds the lexicon and the
romanization anchor, which makes the script-less sources cheap to resolve afterwards. Doing it
in the other order creates a large unknown-token queue that a later source would have answered
for free.

## Conventions

**Telugu script is the anchor.** Where a source supplies script, canonical romanization is
derived from it by `tools/te2rom.py`. A source's own romanization is kept in `raw_rom` and
never used as canonical, so sources cannot drift apart.

`te2rom.py` agrees with the hand-written course spellings on 86% of 364 attested pairs. Every
disagreement was inspected: in essentially all of them the converter is faithful to the script
and the course page had a typo or an inconsistency — `అరుగు` is *arugu* not *aragu*, `కానీ` is
*kānī* not *kāni*, `చిన్నాన్న` is *chinnānna* not *chinnāna*. Deliberate convention choices:
anusvara is written `n`/`m` before a stop and `ṁ` word-final; `ṇḍ` keeps its retroflex; `ṅ` and
`ñ` are written `n`, since "ng" and "nch" already read correctly in English.

**Merge key** is the Telugu script, falling back to folded romanization for script-less
sources. The site wins conflicts — its glosses carry register cues and worked examples. Glosses
from other sources are appended with `;` rather than overwriting.

## Caveats on source 2 (top1000)

It is an **English** frequency list machine-translated into Telugu, not a Telugu frequency
list. That produces three systematic problems, all flagged rather than dropped:

- **12 bound suffixes presented as words** — `లో` glossed "in", `కు` "to", `ది` "the". Telugu
  marks these with case endings; it has no definite article at all, which Lesson 1 already
  teaches. A card reading "in = lō" teaches a preposition the language does not have.
- **82 inflected verb forms presented as headwords** — `అన్నాడు annāḍu` glossed "said" is
  specifically *he* said; `వెళ్ళింది veḷḷindi` "went" is *she/it* went; `చేయండి chēyaṇḍi` "do"
  is the polite imperative. Learned as vocabulary these bake in an agreement error. 34 of them
  were automatically traced back to their dictionary form against the Verb Lab paradigms and
  carry a `lemma`.
- **35 English words respelled in Telugu script** — `సెట్ seṭ` "set", `మాప్ māp` "map". Some
  are genuine loanwords, some are not; the flag means "decide", not "wrong".

Also 1000 rows collapse to 901 distinct forms, because English *very / much / too / quite / lot*
all map to `చాలా chālā`. Only 78 of the site's 271 words appear in it, so the two are
complementary rather than redundant.

**Recommendation:** treat this list as a candidate pool and a frequency signal, not as the
spine. The PDF book — chosen for Telugu learners, with sentences attached — is the better
backbone.
